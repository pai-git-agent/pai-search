# Personal Assistive Intelligence

## Sequence: **2/19**
- Utterance: Personal Assistive Intelligence (PAI) is an architecture that combines general-purpose AI models, goal-directed agents, operational harnesses, and evolving personal context to make AI more useful to an individual over time. It carries relevant understanding across interactions and uses the person’s knowledge, preferences, objectives, constraints, and decision-making principles to interpret requests and support action. Its personal core, portability, and networkability make it a persistent, adaptive intelligence layer centred on the individual. PAI uses context to shape reasoning and execution so recommendations reflect the user’s objectives and judgment and actions respect constraints and authority. It improves by selectively capturing meaningful experience, retaining its rationale, and applying it where relevant. The personal dimension establishes relevance, portability preserves and extends it, and networkability brings in capabilities and experience developed elsewhere. Transferred context requires personal interpretation, shared expertise requires adaptation, and both depend on a harness for context, permissions, execution, and feedback. The individual contributes intent, expertise, experience, and judgment, models and agents contribute reasoning, synthesis, planning, and action, and the harness connects them through memory, tools, orchestration, and oversight. The architecture’s success depends on how well these components work together to help the individual understand situations, make decisions, and accomplish meaningful goals. PAI proposes a continuing relationship between human judgment and machine capability that develops with the individual by carrying forward learning, adapting to changing needs, and expanding what the individual can accomplish through others’ knowledge and capabilities. Realising it requires meaningful memory, selective transfer, trustworthy knowledge exchange, and clear user control.
