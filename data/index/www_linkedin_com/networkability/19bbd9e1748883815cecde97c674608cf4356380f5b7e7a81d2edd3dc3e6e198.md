# Networkability

## Sequence: **8/22**
- Utterance: Networkability connects individuals to people, agents, knowledge sources, reusable workflows, shared expertise, and collective experience. Expertise is distributed, and a personal system cannot derive all specialised knowledge from the user’s experience alone. Shared contributions provide starting capabilities that personal context can shape and adapt. As users apply them, selected refinements can be contributed back with appropriate authorisation and validation. This exchange expands individual capability and improves shared resources. Networks can also distribute unreliable assumptions and unsafe practices, so they require permission and privacy, provenance and attribution, and validation and quality assurance. Reusable methods need to be kept separate from private execution data such as supplier contracts, pricing, and internal business priorities. Adopting a workflow should not automatically give it access or authority, and its requested permissions and actions must remain under the individual’s controls. Under these conditions, participants gain access to broader expertise and validated contributions increase the value available to others.
