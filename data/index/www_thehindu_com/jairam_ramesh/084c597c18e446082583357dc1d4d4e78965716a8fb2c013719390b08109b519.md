# Jairam Ramesh

## Sequence: **6/15**
- Utterance: Congress leader and communications chief Jairam Ramesh alleged on Monday, November 9, 2026, that the 88-day interval since the Lok Sabha’s adjournment sine die, with prorogation still pending, was designed to give Union Home Minister Amit Shah time to muster a two-thirds majority to pass the Delimitation Bills. In a post on X, Ramesh cited page 192 of the eighth edition of Practice and Procedure of Parliament, published in mid-2024, for a usual interval of two to four days and said a footnote recorded a maximum of 28 days during the 2015 monsoon session. Ramesh noted that Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju had cited a gap of 87 days in the 2008 monsoon session in an October 19 letter to Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge. Ramesh said the book’s omission puzzled him and that all parties had agreed to the 2008 arrangement given the circumstances then. He said the new gap set a record even by the minister’s own reckoning and alleged it was intended to help Shah manipulate a two-thirds majority for bills Ramesh described as dangerous and destructive. Ramesh said Opposition unity and solidarity had defeated the government’s plans on April 17, 2026, and would prevail again if Shah used what Ramesh called his “trademark bulldozer tactics.” He said Kharge had written to Prime Minister Narendra Modi four times in the previous six months seeking an all-party meeting to discuss the proposals transparently and in detail before legislation was introduced, and that Modi had repeatedly refused.
